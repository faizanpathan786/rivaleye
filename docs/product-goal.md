# RivalEye: Product Goal

> **Canonical product vision.** Read this before any non-trivial product, scope, or roadmap decision. The repo conventions live in `CLAUDE.md`; the *why* and the *destination* live here.

RivalEye is being built to help SaaS teams understand what users **really think about their competitors**.

Today, competitor research usually focuses on visible surfaces: websites, pricing pages, feature lists, designs, ads, changelogs, and positioning. Designers study competitor UI. PMs compare feature sets. Growth teams study messaging and channels. Founders look for what competitors are doing better or worse.

But one important layer is still hard to see:

**What are the competitor's users actually saying in public?**

Users are already sharing valuable signals across Reddit, forums, reviews, communities, app stores, Product Hunt, Hacker News, and social platforms. They talk about what they love, what frustrates them, what features they want, why pricing feels painful, why they stay, why they leave, and what alternatives they are considering.

RivalEye exists to capture that layer.

## The core goal

**RivalEye helps SaaS teams turn public user conversations about competitors into product, positioning, and growth decisions.**

It is not just a competitor weakness finder.
It is not just a brand monitoring tool.
It is not just an AI report generator.

RivalEye is the **user perception layer of competitor research**.

It answers:

```txt
What do users love about this competitor?
What do users hate or complain about?
What do users wish existed?
Why do users choose this competitor?
Why do users leave or look for alternatives?
What exact language do users use?
Where is the opportunity for us?
```

## The product promise

**See what users really think about your competitors: what they love, what they hate, what they want next, and who may be ready to switch.**

This gives different teams different value:

For founders, RivalEye helps find market openings and sharper wedges.

For PMs, it reveals feature gaps, repeated pain points, and roadmap evidence.

For marketers, it provides real user language, positioning angles, objections, and comparison-page insights.

For growth and sales teams, it surfaces switch-intent conversations and people already looking for alternatives.

## The main signal types

RivalEye should organize competitor intelligence into four core signal types:

### 1. Love Signals

What users like, praise, and stay for.

This helps teams understand the competitor's real strength and avoid blindly attacking the wrong thing.

### 2. Pain Signals

What users complain about repeatedly.

This reveals weaknesses, friction, pricing pain, support issues, UX problems, and unmet expectations.

### 3. Gap Signals

What users ask for but do not get.

This helps product teams identify feature gaps, workflow needs, and possible MVP opportunities.

### 4. Switch Signals

Who is considering alternatives or showing buying intent.

This helps growth teams find conversations worth joining and users who may be open to switching.

## The first product we are building

The first version of RivalEye should be simple:

```txt
Enter a competitor
↓
RivalEye scans public conversations
↓
It finds love, pain, gap, and switch signals
↓
It clusters them into a clear report
↓
It shows quotes, source links, and recommended actions
```

The first goal is not to build a huge dashboard or monitor every platform.

The first goal is:

**Generate one genuinely useful competitor perception report that helps a SaaS team decide what to build, say, or target next.**

## North star

**RivalEye's north star is to help teams make better product, positioning, and growth decisions from real user perception of competitors.**

A good RivalEye report should make the user say:

```txt
Now I understand where this competitor is strong, where users are frustrated, what the market wants, and what opportunity we can attack.
```

That is what we are building.
